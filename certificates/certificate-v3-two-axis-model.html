June 2026  ·  Two-axis model: language efficiency × algorithmic efficiency  ·  arXiv 2410.05460

The question this version answers: When we bring the language efficiency table alongside our certificates, does it change the ratings?

Short answer: yes, but clarifying rather than overturning. It adds a second axis — the language multiplier — that sits beneath the algorithmic rating. It reveals the two certificates are not directly comparable on the same scale, and shows that the GenAI stack has a structural energy ceiling the RPG stack doesn't have.

The stack is correctly layered: the most computationally intensive work (LLM inference) runs in the most energy-efficient language (C++). The least intensive work (UI rendering) runs in the least efficient language (TypeScript). This is the right architecture even if it wasn't consciously designed that way.

Priority order for energy improvement: (1) Algorithmic changes first — highest leverage, always improvable. (2) Language choice second — only worth changing if compute volume is large and sustained. (3) Infrastructure last.
