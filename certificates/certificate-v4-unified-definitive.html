Responsibob is a developer-level code energy efficiency framework operating in the "develop efficiently" band of the Defra carbon reduction curve (20% potential saving). It uses a two-axis model — language multiplier × algorithmic efficiency — to produce A–G ratings grounded in real source code evidence.

Core principle: Good engineering decisions and responsible computing decisions are usually the same decision. The SAMCO RLA→SQL transformation was driven by performance. The PassportEye choice was driven by demo speed. Neither was driven by energy — but both were the right energy choice. Making that visible is what Responsibob does.

Jevons' Paradox — A Nuanced Risk, Not a Uniform Warning

Jevons' Paradox: efficiency improvements that make a resource cheaper to use can lead to increased consumption — if there is latent demand waiting to be served. But the risk is not uniform across all contexts.

✓ Low risk — SAMCO (short term)

An operational system not at capacity ceiling will not spontaneously run 300× more queries. If today's utilisation is 40%, making queries 300× faster does not fill that gap immediately. The efficiency gain is largely a real saving.

Monitor over 1–2 years: efficiency may enable new features that add load over time — not Jevons' Paradox in the classical sense, but worth tracking.

⚠ Higher risk — GenAI inference

When AI inference gets cheaper, organisations tend to expand the scope of what they ask AI to do — not just do the same work for less. This is new demand created by lower cost, not latent demand served.

Cheaper tokens → more calls per session → new use cases enabled. The rebound effect here is more active than in traditional software.

✓ Negligible — PassportEye

Bounded by the number of passports processed. You cannot "use more CV" to parse an MRZ than you would with a vision model. Task volume is determined by business process, not by compute cost. Clean saving with no rebound.

⚠ Watch — new features enabled by headroom

SAMCO ART400's speed may enable integrations or features previously impractical. This is not Jevons' Paradox classically — it is value creation from efficiency — but it does mean net consumption may not fall by the full 99.7%.

The correct framing: certificates measure per-transaction efficiency. Volume trending (DevOps Velocity) confirms net benefit.

Responsibob Positioning Statement (v4)

Responsibob is a developer-level code energy efficiency framework that operates in the "develop efficiently" band of the Defra carbon reduction curve (20% potential saving at DWP scale: ~£200M of a £1B/year ICT estate). It uses a two-axis model — language multiplier (structural, from arXiv 2410.05460) × algorithmic efficiency (variable, improvable) — to produce A–G ratings grounded in real source code evidence.

It is complementary to infrastructure-level efficiency frameworks (Home Office Hosting Cost Efficiency Rating, Turbonomic ARM) and policy-level decisions (design to use less, design & develop nothing). Together these three layers address the full carbon reduction potential across a software estate.

The critical constraint is Jevons' Paradox — but the risk is context-dependent: low for operational systems not at capacity ceiling, higher for GenAI inference where cheaper tokens tend to unlock more usage, negligible for bounded deterministic tasks. Per-transaction certificates must be paired with volume trending to confirm net benefit.

The unifying principle: good engineering and responsible computing are usually the same decision. The value of Responsibob is making that visible — at the point of code creation, in the CI/CD pipeline, and as a certifiable, improvable property of every modernisation engagement.
